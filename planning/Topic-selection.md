# Topic : Time-Series Traffic & Network Load Forecasting. 

# Some Important points 
1. Evaluation metrics and thesis focus on forecasting future network metrics (like bandwidth availability, jitter trends, and traffic spikes) using machine learning models.
2. BASICALLY : "We aren't just building a file-transfer app. We are building a machine learning engine that predicts future internet slowdowns and network traffic jams before they actually happen. The file transfer part is just how we test the model in real-time."

# Literature survey ( sort of )
## 1. Deep Learning for Time Series Forecasting: A Survey" (IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems)
### > What it covers: The ultimate rulebook proving that neural networks (like LSTMs) are way better at guessing future numbers than old-school math formulas (like ARIMA).
### > How you use it:  Your baseline proof that your AI model is legit.


## 2. Spatial-Temporal Network Traffic Forecasting via Deep Learning 
### What it means: Looking at past router logs to predict when a network is about to choke.
### Your use case: Explains how your system reads old data to predict future traffic jams.

## 3. Predictive Algorithms for Symmetric NAT Traversal Using Port Allocation Functions (Recent IEEE | ResearchGate Benchmark)
### What it means: Internet routers shuffle ports randomly, but that randomness has math behind it.
### Your use case: The mathematical backup for why your model can guess future network paths.

## 4. A Machine Learning Framework for Real-Time Network Congestion Prediction and Flow Control (Journal of Network and Computer Applications)
### What it means: Embedding an AI model directly into a live system to stop lag spikes milliseconds before they hit.
### Your use case: Proves your app actually uses the predictions to do something cool on the fly.

## 5. Concept Drift Adaptation in Network Traffic Time-Series Forecasting" (ACM Computing Surveys)
### What it means: AI models get dumb over time because networks change. This paper teaches the model how to keep learning on the fly.
### Your use case: Shows the professors your project isn't a static toy—it adapts to real-world chaos.
